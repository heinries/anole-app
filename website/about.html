HOUSTON HACKATHON 2026 / THE IDEA

A picture tells a thousand words. Let's tell our story.

Anole helps communities turn what they notice into useful local knowledge. Choose a topic, capture an observation, and help others see what is happening around them.

It started with 15 pitches

At the launch of Houston Hackathon 2026, we listened to 15 pitches about improving life in the city. People wanted to find relief from the heat, discover accessible places, care for public spaces, spot wildlife, and report maintenance issues.

The ideas were different, but the first step was often the same: someone had to notice something, capture where it was, and share it with others. We imagined one flexible place where a community could do that for any topic it cared about. That idea became Anole.

Why Anole?

Anoles are small lizards that pause, look around, and notice their surroundings. Our mascot, Bole, is a brown anole with a raised tail: a little signal that something nearby deserves attention.

Bole reminds us to look for more than problems. A shaded seat, a welcoming space, or a solution someone has tried can be worth sharing too. Anole is about capturing those observations so a community can learn from them.

Start small. Make it useful.

Pick a topic that matters to your community. Register it, invite people to contribute a capture, and build a map of what they discover. A useful local story can begin with one observation.
